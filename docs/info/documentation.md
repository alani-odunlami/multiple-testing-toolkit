# Technical Problem-Solving Brief

## 1. Problem statement
What problem am I trying to solve, and why does it matter?

## 2. Desired outcome
What should the solution produce? How will success be measured?

## 3. Known facts
What evidence, data, documentation and constraints do I already have?

## 4. Assumptions and unknowns
What am I assuming? What must I investigate or clarify?

## 5. Scope and constraints
What is included or excluded? Consider runtime, memory, accuracy, cost, security and maintainability.

## 6. Problem decomposition
Break the problem into smaller components. For each component, specify its inputs, outputs, dependencies and responsibilities.

## 7. Candidate solutions
What approaches could work? What are their advantages, disadvantages and trade-offs?

## 8. Risks and edge cases
What could fail? What unusual inputs or scenarios must be handled?

## 9. Experiment and validation plan
What is the smallest useful experiment? What tests and evidence will establish correctness?

## 10. Implementation plan
List the tasks in dependency order, with clear completion criteria.

## 11. Results and decision
What did the evidence show? Why was the final approach selected?

## 12. Review
Does the solution satisfy the original requirements? What remains uncertain or needs improvement?
